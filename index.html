Complete rule analysis – 10 assessment areas

RRuleLens will organize the analysis into rule identification, business purpose, technical context, execution, logic, actions, assignment, dependencies, testing and impact. Missing configuration details will be explicitly marked as unavailable rather than guessed.

1. Rule identification

Analyze a rule to populate its identity and ownership details.

2. Business purpose

Business reason, requirement, automation and failure consequence.

3. Technical context

Base object, scenario, parameters and referenced objects.

4. Trigger / execution point

Execution event and processing action.

5. IF condition

Conditions, operators, comparisons and branches.

6. THEN / ELSE actions

Actions and outcomes performed by the rule.

7. Rule assignment

Assignment location, object, field and event.

8. Dependencies

Referenced objects, upstream rules and downstream effects.

9. Testing and exceptions

Positive, negative, boundary and exception scenarios.

10. Impact and maintenance

Business impact, risks, duplication, audit and ownership.

Evidence convention: “Found in source” means visible in the supplied text/OCR; “Inferred” means a cautious interpretation; “Not available” means this screenshot or text does not provide enough evidence. Assignment, owner, effective date and audit history generally require configuration or administrative evidence.

Rule explanation order – quick review

The following is a reusable explanation checklist. The Event Reason example is illustrative only; it is not the interpretation of the sick-leave rule.

1. What does the rule do?Example: Automatically derives Event Reason.
2. Why is it required?Example: Avoids manual selection.
3. When does it execute?Example: When Job Information is saved.
4. What does it check?Example: Company, Department and Event.
5. What action does it perform?Example: Sets Event Reason to Transfer.
6. Where is it assigned?Example: Job Information – onSave.
7. What are the dependencies?Example: Company and Department values.
8. What happens if conditions fail?Example: No event reason is derived.
9. What is the business impact?Example: Incorrect event reason may affect downstream processing.
10. How is it tested?Example: Test transfers for different departments and companies.
